What does it mean to "see"?

Before we look at how machines do it, consider how you do it. Two very different modes of perception underpin human vision — and they map precisely onto the two major paradigms of computer vision.

Two modes of human perception
📖
Reading a page of text
Your eyes move word by word, left to right, top to bottom. You process small chunks at a time. Understanding builds from these local pieces, one step at a time.
→ Maps to: Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
🏖️
Looking at a beach photograph
You take in the entire scene at once — ocean, sand, person, seagull. You instantly understand relationships between distant objects: the seagull is above the water, the person is on the sand.
→ Maps to: Vision Transformer (ViT)
These two modes of perception — local sequential processing versus global holistic understanding — are exactly the two paradigms that define how neural networks learn to see.

The two paradigms

Click either paradigm to explore how it works and what makes it distinctive.

Paradigm 1
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
Processes images through small local filters that slide across the image — much like reading word by word. Builds understanding from local features upward.
Paradigm 2
Vision Transformer (ViT)
Divides the image into patches and processes them all at once, computing attention between every pair — much like taking in a photograph holistically.

How CNNs process an image

CNNs learn to see by scanning small local regions — filters slide across the image extracting features, then stack those features into increasingly abstract representations.

Diagram: data flow through a CNN — the highlighted blue stage corresponds to the current step

How Vision Transformers process an image

ViTs learn to see by treating an image as a sequence of patches — like words in a sentence — and computing attention between every patch simultaneously.

Local vs global — the core difference

The fundamental difference between the two paradigms comes down to receptive field: how much of the image does each operation "see" at once?

What is a receptive field? The receptive field of a neuron is simply the region of the original image that influences its output — the pixels it can "see." A small receptive field means the neuron only knows about a tiny local patch. A large receptive field means it can draw on information from across the entire image.

CNN vs ViT — summary

A quick reference across the key dimensions that distinguish the two paradigms.

Dimension CNN Vision Transformer (ViT)
Core operation Convolution (sliding filter) Self-attention (patch pairs)
Receptive field Local → global (layered) Global from layer 1
Inductive bias Strong (translation equivariance) Weak (learned from data)
Data efficiency Works well with less data Needs large datasets
Long-range dependencies Hard — requires many layers Native — computed in every layer
Computational cost Efficient (linear in image size) Quadratic in number of patches
Best known for Image classification, object detection, segmentation Large-scale vision, vision-language models
Iconic models AlexNet · ResNet · EfficientNet ViT · DeiT · CLIP · SAM
Neither paradigm has "won." Modern architectures increasingly combine both: hybrid models use CNN layers for early feature extraction and transformer layers for global reasoning. The two paradigms are complementary, not competing.
